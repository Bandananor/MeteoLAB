A guide for newcomers to the site

First steps

Step by step: how to open the simulator, run your first storm and find any button. The steps for a computer and for a phone are given separately. If some words are unclear (CAPE, wind shear, mesocyclone), they are explained in the “Meteorology from zero” guide. At the very end is the “Where to find…” table: every feature and the way to it.

1Before you start

  • Browser: a recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. A graphics card with WebGL is needed (almost everywhere has one).
  • Computer or phone: the model is computed on your device — faster and with more to see on a computer; a phone copes too but may get warm.
  • Nothing to install, no sign-up. Everything you do stays with you until you save a file or send a link yourself.
  • Model time runs faster than real time: 8 times by default (×8). A storm grows over 20–30 model minutes — 3–4 minutes of real time.

2A first experiment on a computer

  1. Open the simulator (on the home page — the “Launch the simulator” button or the “Simulator” item in the header).
  2. On the first visit a “How should MeteoLab look?” window appears — press “Computer” (the option that fits your screen is marked). An offer to look at the guide may follow — you can close it.
  3. The run is already going: at the top a green “Running” label and the model’s clock T+. The “Summer day” scenario is selected by default.
  4. In the left column, in “Scenarios”, press “Classic supercell”. The run starts again from T+00:00.
  5. Watch the 3D view in the centre. In 5–10 model minutes a white cloud rises, by minutes 15–20 it grows into an anvil, by minutes 20–30 the downpour and the lightning begin, and a column of yellow rings rises in the storm — the mesocyclone.
  6. Turn the camera: hold the left mouse button and drag — the view rotates; the right button pans; the wheel — closer / further.
  7. On the right watch “Storm mode” (what is happening, in words) and the big numbers: updraught, rotation, rain, gust, hail.
  8. Want it faster — find the “Computation” group on the left (at the bottom of the settings, open it with a click) and the “Time acceleration” slider. Pause — the “Pause” button at the bottom of the left column.
Keys

R — radar, S — Skew-T, Z — zones, L — lightning, M — station, Esc — close an instrument. A double click on the ground — a new warm bubble (thermal) there.

3A first experiment on a phone

  1. Open the simulator. In the “How should MeteoLab look?” window press “Phone”.
  2. The 3D view fills the screen. At the top — the model time, updraught, rain and rotation in large numbers, below them the storm mode.
  3. At the bottom — the bottom bar of six buttons: “Scenarios”, “Pause”, “Restart”, “Speed”, “Data”, “Instruments”.
  4. Press “Scenarios” — a sheet slides up from below. In it press “Classic supercell”. The sheet closes with the “Hide” button at its top or a tap on the 3D view above it.
  5. Watch the storm grow. Gestures: one finger — rotate the view, a two-finger pinch — closer / further, a double tap on the ground — a new thermal.
  6. “Speed” cycles the acceleration: ×4 → ×8 → ×16 → ×30 → ×60.
  7. “Data” opens a sheet with the numbers, the “Storm history” chart, the indices and the hodograph — scroll it with a finger.
  8. “Instruments” opens a menu: Radar, Skew-T, Zones, Lightning, Station, switching to the computer layout, “How to use it”, the project’s channel, the language and the theme (light / dark).

4A tour of the screen

On a computer the screen has four parts:

Top line
on the left — the logo (leads to the home page), the run’s status, the T+ clock, the number of computing threads, the domain size. Then — the instrument buttons “Radar”, “Skew-T”, “Zones”, “Lightning”, “Station”, the “Guide” link (this guide), the language switch (EN / RU), “Phone layout” and the theme “Auto / Light / Dark”.
Left column
from top to bottom: “Scenarios” (buttons of ready-made weather), the chosen one’s description (with the “More” button and the CAPE, CIN, shear and SRH numbers), “Real sounding…”, then the groups of settings: “Temperature profile”, “Humidity by layer”, “Wind profile”, “3D dynamics”, “Surface and radiation”, “Computation”, “What is computed and what is not”. At the very bottom — “Restart”, “Pause”, “Link to the run”, “Save”, “Open…”, “Go back to minute…”.
Centre
above the 3D view — the field tabs: “Clouds”, “Vertical motion”, “Temperature”, “Moisture”, “Vorticity”, “Rotation (UH)”, “Cold pool”. On the view itself, top left — the toggles “Flow”, “Snow and rain”, “Mesocyclone”, “Structure”, “Lightning”, “Rain total”. At the bottom — a mouse hint and a line with the grid, the step and where the domain is moving.
Right column
“Storm mode”, “Cells” (a numbered list of storms and the “Whole domain” button), “Values over the whole domain” (six big numbers), “Storm history” (five charts and a CSV button), then the groups “Instability indices”, “Environment: sounding and levels”, “Wind: hodograph and shear”, “All the storm’s values”, “What is happening”.

On a phone the left column is the “Scenarios” sheet, the right one the “Data” sheet, the instrument buttons are in the “Instruments” menu, the field tabs are a strip under the top line (scroll it sideways with a finger).

Many numbers have an i button: press it for an explanation of what it is and where it comes from.

5Changing the weather

  1. Choose “Summer day”.
  2. Open the “Temperature profile” group (on a phone — in the “Scenarios” sheet, below the scenario’s description).
  3. Move “Surface temperature” 3 °C higher. Look at the CAPE and CIN numbers under the scenario’s description — they changed at once.
  4. Release the slider — it has a ↻ mark: the run starts again with the new weather. Settings marked ↻ change the atmosphere itself, so the experiment restarts; those without the mark (time of day, sun, acceleration) act at once.
  5. Now open “Wind profile” and raise “Wind at 6 km” to 25–30 m/s. On the right (on a phone — “Data”), in the “Wind: hodograph and shear” group, the hodograph’s line stretches and “0–6 km wind shear” grows — with such shear the storm tilts and may start to rotate.
  6. The wind can also be set right on the hodograph: drag the circle at the height you want with a mouse or a finger.

What exactly to turn to get a supercell, a microburst or large hail is in the “Experiments and recipes” guide.

6Looking inside the storm

  1. Above the 3D view choose the “Vertical motion” tab. Coloured slices appear: red — the air rises, blue — it sinks.
  2. At the top right of the view — the field panel with the colour scale (on a phone — above the view; the slices and the volume are under the “Settings ▾” button). The “Horizontal slice height” slider raises the slice; “Cross-section north ↔ south” moves the vertical section.
  3. “Display” → “Volume only” shows the field as a coloured cloud instead of slices.
  4. The “Rotation (UH)” tab — where the rising air rotates; its settings have a choice of layer (2–5 km — the mesocyclone, 0–1 km — rotation near the ground).
  5. Back to the ordinary view — the “Clouds” tab.
  6. Pick one storm: click a cloud (or a cell’s number in the list on the right) — the numbers on the right become that cell’s. “Whole domain” — back.

7Instruments

Each instrument is a separate screen over the 3D view. Close it with “Close” at the top right or Esc. On a computer the instrument buttons are in the top line, on a phone under “Instruments”.

Radar (R)
a weather radar screen. On the right: the product (Z — echo strength, V and SRV — velocity, CR, ZDR, ρhv, KDP), the beam tilt, a vertical cross-section, the palette, the grid, mesocyclones. A click on the screen moves the radar; a section is drawn through any two points of the map (the ends can be dragged), Shift + click — from the radar through a point.
Skew-T (S)
a thermodynamic diagram — the temperature and humidity profile, the parcel’s path, CAPE and CIN. “Probe in the model” — a profile taken right from the storm.
Zones (Z)
a map for uneven weather: “Draw a zone” → clicks on the map → “Done” → the temperature and moisture sliders → “Apply — restart the run”. The lines show where storms will go.
Lightning (L)
a lightning detector: a map of strokes and flashes, “Density”, a period of 10 / 30 / 60 min, a chart of the rate and the jumps; a click on a mark — the flash’s card.
Station (M)
“Place with a click in 3D” → the screen closes → click the ground in the storm’s path — the station’s screen opens by itself with a meteogram: temperature, pressure, wind, precipitation. “Export CSV” — a table.

8Save and share

  • “Link to the run” (at the bottom of the left column; on a phone — at the very bottom of the “Scenarios” sheet) copies an address. Send it — the recipient gets the same experiment with the same numbers.
  • “Save” — a .meteolab file with the whole run (~4 MB); “Open…” — continue it from the same minute.
  • “Go back to minute…” — a drop-down list: the model keeps its state every 5 model minutes; choose a minute and the run goes on from there. You can change a setting and compare what changed.
  • “CSV” by the “Storm history” chart and at the station — the data as a table for a spreadsheet.

10If something is wrong

A black screen instead of 3D
the browser may have taken the graphics card away from the page (often on a phone short of memory) — a message with a reload button appears. Close other tabs and reload.
Everything is slow
under “Time acceleration” it says how much the computer actually manages. What helps: the standard domain, “Transport: fast”, a smaller acceleration, closing other tabs.
The cloud does not grow
wait 10 model minutes; look at the CIN under the scenario’s description — a strong “cap” may keep the storm out (that is a result too). In “Diurnal cycle” and “Loaded gun” the storms come only after noon by the model’s clock.
The sliders do not move the weather
the “WK supercell” and a loaded sounding have their profile set not by the sliders (the groups are dimmed); choose another scenario.
I could not find a button
see the “Where to find…” table above or ask in the project’s channel.
