Manual

How to use it

The simulator is a thunderstorm model computed right in your browser. You set the weather (the profile of temperature, humidity and wind by height, the surface, the time of day) and the model solves the equations and shows what grows out of it. Below — where the buttons are and how to run your own experiments.

How to use it (this page)a reference to the whole interface

1Quick start

  1. Open the simulator. On the first visit it asks for a layout — computer or phone; later the layout is switched with the “Phone layout” button (on a phone — in the “Instruments” menu).
  2. On the left, in “Scenarios”, choose ready-made weather — for example, “Classic supercell”. The run restarts at once.
  3. Watch: in 5–10 model minutes a cloud rises, by minutes 20–30 a storm with a downpour. The model’s clock is T+ at the top. Faster or slower — the “Time acceleration” slider in the “Computation” group (on a phone — the “Speed” button).
  4. “Pause” stops the run, “Restart” starts the same experiment from the beginning (each start is slightly different: small random perturbations at the ground).
Scenarios without a thermal

“Diurnal cycle” and “Loaded gun” start in the morning and wait for the sun to heat the air: for the first hours almost nothing happens. Raise the acceleration — the storms come after noon.

2What is where on the screen

Top line
the status and the model time T+ (hours:minutes:seconds since the start of the experiment) and the model’s local time of day, the number of computing threads and the domain size; menus by task: Run (restart, pause — the Space key, a sounding, open and save a file, a link), Instruments (Doppler radar R, Skew-T S, environment zones Z, lightning detector L, surface analysis A, weather satellite I, weather stations M), Experiments (comparing A/B — C, ensemble — E, regime map — K, intervening by hand — H), Export (the “Storm history” CSV, a PNG snapshot — P, an MP4 video — V); each item shows its key; the layout switch, the language switch (EN / RU) and the theme (Auto / Light / Dark).
Left column — initial conditions
where the environment comes from: the scenarios, the synoptic situations and “Your own environment” (a real sounding, building your own situation); the chosen one’s description (“More” — the full text); “Starting environment” — ML CAPE and CIN, LCL, LFC, 0–6 km shear, DCAPE, SRH 0–1 and 0–3 km (the mixed-layer parcel, MetPy conventions): pinned over the groups, in view while scrolling and recounted while a slider moves; below — the settings in groups; at the bottom — “Restart”, “Pause” and the rewind “Go back to minute…” (save, open and the link are in the “Run” menu).
Centre
the 3D view of the storm; above it the field tabs (clouds, vertical motion, temperature, moisture, vorticity, rotation, cold pool), on the view the toggles “Flow”, “Snow and rain”, “Mesocyclone”, “Rain total”.
Right column
what the storm is doing now (“Storm mode”), the list of cells, the main numbers, the “Storm history” chart and groups with the indices, the sounding, the hodograph and all the values.

Many numbers and labels have an i button or a tooltip on hover — it briefly explains what it is and where it comes from.

3Changing the settings

The first group is “Main parameters”: a few knobs, each changing the whole profile wherever it comes from (the sliders, a sounding or the WK formulas): heat (the air near the ground warmer or cooler than the scenario’s, up to 1.5 km), moisture of the lowest 1.5 km (a share of the scenario’s vapour), wind aloft (the 0–6 km shear), wind turning 0–3 km (clockwise — helicity). The WK formulas have no heat and no turning, a sounding no turning. Then the groups in the order an environment is described: temperature, humidity, wind, surface and radiation, initiation, dynamics, advection, numerics.

All the settings are sliders in the left column, arranged in groups; a group opens with a click on its title.

  • The ↻ mark by a name — changing it restarts the run: it is the atmosphere itself (temperature, humidity, wind, latitude, surface, city, thermal strength). Without the mark — it acts at once on the running experiment: time of day, sun, soil moisture, turbulence, acceleration.
  • Temperature profile: the surface temperature and how fast the air cools with height in three layers (the lapse rate, K/km — the larger, the more unstable), the tropopause height, and the cap — an inversion (a “lid”) that holds convection back until something breaks through it.
  • Humidity by layer: the relative humidity at the ground and above; the “moist mixed layer” keeps the moisture at the ground uniform up to a given height, as on a hot day.
  • Wind profile: the speed and direction at six heights. The direction is where it blows from, as in meteorology (270° — a westerly). The shear and turning of the wind with height are what matters for rotation: strong 0–6 km shear and a wind veering (turning clockwise) near the ground give supercells. The hodograph on the right shows the result at once, and its circles (0, 0.5, 1, 3, 6 and 10 km) can be dragged with a mouse or a finger — they are the same wind sliders, only clearer.
  • Surface and radiation: the surface type, the time of day and the day of the year (they give the sun’s height), the sun’s strength, the soil moisture, a city in the centre of the domain.
  • Initiation: the starting thermal strength (0 — no thermal: only the sun starts convection); more thermals — a double click on the ground or “Intervene by hand”.
  • Dynamics: Coriolis and turbulence: the latitude (the Coriolis parameter) and the subgrid turbulence factor.
  • Advection: the environment changes — how the wind brings different air over hours. The ready-made buttons (“Moisture inflow”, “Cold aloft (trough)”, “Warm air above the layer”, “Dry air in the middle”) add a change; “Add a change” — your own. Each change has: what changes (temperature in K or water vapour in g/kg), by how much, in which layer (from and to, km), when it starts and over how many hours it arrives; below it — the same in words. The chart below shows how CAPE and CIN of the surface parcel will change by the hour (without the sun and the storms themselves). Any change restarts the run. The model changes only the temperature and the moisture: the lift ahead of a trough and changes of the wind are not included. In the ready-made stories the changes are sped up several times to fit into 2–3 hours: in nature a trough brings 2–4 K over 6–12 hours, warm air 0.2–0.5 K an hour. The height matters: warm air above the mixed layer (2–3.5 km in the daytime) strengthens the “cap”, below it warms the whole layer, like the sun.
  • Numerics and computation: the transport (accurate or fast), “The domain moves with the storm”, the domain size (large — roomier but ~4 times slower; “Custom…” — width and depth by sliders; very large domains are slow until the computation moves to the GPU), time acceleration up to ×120 (below it — how much the computer actually manages), the microphysics (with ice or warm rain only).

Under the scenario’s description — its ML CAPE (the instability energy), CIN (how much the “cap” holds back), 0–6 km shear and SRH 0–1 km (the wind’s twist near the ground). They are recomputed as you move the sliders — so you see where a change leads before the run.

Synoptic situations — under the scenarios: 2–3 hour stories in which the weather changes by itself. “Warm sector and cold front” — moisture arrives, the front breaks the cap, rotating storms on it; “Upper trough” — the sun starts storms, the cold aloft keeps them going; “Warm air above the layer: no storms” — the same day, but warm air aloft locks convection in. Under the description — the story by the hour: the local time and what happens; the current stage is highlighted, the past ones darker. An acceleration of ×30–×60 helps get through a story in a few minutes.

Build your own situation — in the same place, under the situation buttons: choose an air mass (maritime tropical, maritime polar, continental, dry steppe), what sets off convection (sun only, a cold or warm front, an upper trough, warm moist air arriving, warm air above the layer), the wind shear (weak, moderate, strong) and the start hour — “Build and run”. The front is set up to enter the domain after half an hour and reach the middle by an hour and a half; with a front a 1.5 K cap is added, otherwise the sun would start storms ahead of the front. Honestly: the CIN of such a cap is only ~10 J/kg, in nature it would not hold; in the model it does, because on a ~1 km grid the sun starts convection slowly. Real caps will be calibrated on a 500 m grid. What you built can then be changed by the sliders, on the “Zones” screen and in the “Advection” group.

Real sounding. The “Real sounding…” button under the scenarios (on a phone — in the “Scenarios” sheet): load a file or paste a sounding’s text — a University of Wyoming page (“Text: List”), SHARPpy, CM1/WRF input_sounding or CSV. Before the run you see the profile, CAPE, the shear and what was fixed in the data; you set the thermal yourself. A link to such an experiment and a .meteolab file carry the sounding too.

After the start the temperature, humidity and wind sliders are dimmed with a “sounding” mark: the profile comes from the data. The thermal, the sun, the surface and the computation settings work. The sounding’s wind can be adjusted on the hodograph: drag a circle — the wind shifts at its height and fades out smoothly towards the neighbouring circles, the temperature and humidity stay as in the data; “Restore the wind” undoes it. To go back to the sliders, choose any scenario. Remember: the model takes one profile for the whole domain — this is “a storm in the atmosphere of that day”, not a forecast of the day (no fronts, terrain or change of air).

WK supercell

This scenario’s profile is set by the formulas of the classic Weisman–Klemp experiment: the temperature, humidity and wind sliders have no effect on it (the groups are dimmed). To change the profile by hand, take the “Classic supercell” — it is on the ordinary sliders.

Front — on the “Zones” screen, the “Front” section: “Cold” or “Warm”, the contrast (how much colder the cold air is), the depth of the cold air, the slope of the frontal surface (1 : 80 — 1 km of rise over 80 km), the speed, where it is heading and where it starts. On the map — the front’s symbols and where it will be in 20 and 40 minutes, in 3D — the front’s line on the ground. A cold front lifts the warm air on its “head” and can break the cap; at a warm front the warm air flows up over the cold. A ready example is the “Cold front” scenario.

Environment zones (the “Zones” button, key Z; on a phone — in the “Instruments” menu). The environment can be uneven: draw a patch of ground on the map with clicks and set how much warmer or colder, moister or drier the near-surface air is there. Next to it you see CAPE and CIN in the zone and around it. The lines on the map show where storms from the thermal will go in 40 minutes: place a zone on the path, some 30–40 minutes ahead. “Apply” restarts the run. A storm entering a warm moist zone strengthens, a dry one weakens it; a breeze rises at the edges of a warm zone, and storms can start there too. The zones work with a loaded sounding as well and go into the link and the file.

4The 3D view and fields

  • Camera: left mouse button — rotate, right — pan, wheel — zoom. On a phone — a finger, a pinch, two fingers.
  • A double click on the ground (on a phone — a double tap) puts a warm bubble there (+3 K and a push upwards): this is how you start a new cell next to a storm.
  • “By hand” (H) intervenes as the run goes: choose in the menu a thermal, “Warm” / “Cool” (±2 K near the ground), “Moisten” / “Dry” (±2 g/kg of vapour, at most 95 % humidity), “Cold pool” (−6 K in the lowest ~700 m — it spreads as a gust front) or “Vortex” (an anticlockwise spin, 10 m/s) and a strength, then click the ground in the view from above — each touch lays a patch of ~5 km. Esc or “Turn off” — a click chooses a cell again. The menu and the hint show how much heat and vapour were put in by hand this run, beside the rain fallen: what came from outside and what the storm did itself.
  • Field tabs above the view: “Clouds” — the ordinary view; the others show a field in colour — as a horizontal slice at a chosen height, a vertical cross-section and a volume. The slice and volume settings and the colour scale are in the panel at the top right (on a phone — “Settings ▾”).
  • Toggles on the view: “Flow” — particles carried by the wind; “Snow and rain” — falling precipitation (snow melts into rain below the 0 °C level); “Mesocyclone” — a column of rings where the updraught rotates (how deep it goes and whether it reaches the ground); “Lightning” — the flashes’ channels (in-cloud purple, to ground yellow-white); “Rain total” — how much has fallen on the ground.
  • Cells: every real storm has a number above its cloud. A click on the cloud or on the number in the list on the right selects the cell: the numbers on the right become its numbers; “Whole domain” brings back the maxima over the whole domain.
  • By default the domain moves with the storm so that it does not leave over the edge: that is why the ground and the grid on it slowly creep. The weather station and the city stay on the ground; with a front (whose domain edges are not periodic) the domain may leave them behind — then untick “The domain moves with the storm”.
  • The sky and the time of day follow the model’s clock: the sun sets, the clouds turn pink at sunset, the anvil still glows after the sun has set for the ground, then twilight and a night with stars (over a city — light pollution from below). In the evening the picture darkens, as for an adapting eye. The haze comes from the humidity at the ground. With a field on the screen the sky is muted to dark blue so the field’s colours read.
  • Rain shafts and hail follow the amount of precipitation in the model: a dense downpour is opaque over ~1 km. Opposite the sun there may be a rainbow in sunlit rain (and a pale second one). The ground darkens from the fallen rain and dries, hail lies white and melts; at sea — foam and a glint from the wind.
  • “From the ground” (G) opens a menu of where the observer stands: “Beside the storm, 25 km” — the whole storm in view, under a clear sky on the inflow side, the sun behind you; “Closer, 12 km” — the updraught’s base, rain shafts and the anvil overhead; “At the weather station” — where the station stands (if placed); “Pick a place…” — click the ground in the 3D view where to stand (Esc — cancel); “Back to the view from above”. In place, dragging turns the head, the wheel zooms, a double click on the ground walks there (no thermal is placed in this view). You stand still while the storm moves — it may leave.
  • “Snapshot” (P; on a phone — the “Instruments” menu) saves the 3D view as a PNG image, without the panels. Every instrument’s header — radar, Skew-T, station and the others — has the same button: it saves the instrument’s screen.
  • “Video” (V; on a phone — the “Instruments” menu) records the 3D view as MP4. It is not a screen capture: the run pauses, the model steps through each frame’s time and the view is drawn at the chosen size (720p … 4K, 16:9 or the screen’s shape) and the high quality however long a frame takes. So the video plays evenly — 30 or 60 frames per second — even from a weak phone that itself draws two frames a second; the recording then takes longer than the video. You choose how much model time passes per second of video (from 10 s — slowed down — to 5 min) and the camera: as now, an orbit round the storm (a turn per minute of video) or from the ground. When a frame is shorter than the model’s step (3 s), the frames blend the neighbouring model states smoothly. Lightning in the video runs in real time. “Stop and save” keeps what is recorded so far, “Discard” throws it away. It needs a browser with WebCodecs: Chrome, Edge, Safari 16.4+, Firefox 130+.
  • “Full screen” (F) hides the panels and buttons; come back with the corner button, Esc or F. In full screen the “Camera” button in the opposite corner sets the exposure, brightness, contrast and saturation of the frame; they are remembered and go into snapshots.
  • Picture quality — the badge in the corner of the 3D view (on a phone — the “Instruments” menu): “Auto” lowers the quality by itself when frames come slower than ~22 a second. On low the picture is softer, but everything important is visible.
  • What is only drawn. Detail smaller than ~1 km — the billows, the bands on the flanks, mammatus, the anvil’s fibres, rain streaks, fields and city blocks — is noise tied to the model’s fields, not computation. Cloud shadows on the ground, the night and the wet ground are only a picture too: the model heats the ground as if there were no clouds, does not cool the air at night, and the soil moisture does not change from rain. In the view from above the air is drawn half as hazy as it really is (the camera is far away), there are no clouds beyond the domain’s edge. Snow falling out of the anvil outside the cloud above the melting level is drawn five times more transparent than the model computes it: in the model it evaporates too slowly and would hang as a dense wall (to be fixed in the physics). The lightning channels’ colours are conventional marks: in-cloud purple, to ground yellow-white; a real channel is blue-white, and in-cloud lightning is mostly seen as the cloud flashing.

5The data on the right

  • Storm mode — in words, what is happening: thermals, a single cell, a multicell storm, a supercell, the outflow taking over. The alarm colour and the “mesocyclone” badge appear only when the core’s rotation has held for 10 minutes. The core rotation threshold (0.003 1/s) is three times lower than the observational one (~0.01 1/s): on a ~1 km grid the vortex is smeared and weaker than a real one. Below — the type of the tallest cloud by the international classification: Cu hum, Cu med, Cu cong, Cb calv (the top glaciated, colder than −20 °C) and Cb cap with an anvil. Under the rain rate and the hail — in words: from “slight” to “violent rain showers”, from “small” to “very large” hail. When “Auto” lowers the picture quality, the badge says “quality lowered: slow frames” for a few seconds.
  • The main numbers: the strongest updraught, the rotation (UH 2–5 km), the rain in the core now and the total at the wettest point, the surface gust, hail. The core passes over one point in 5–15 minutes, so 100 mm/h in the core gives that point 5–20 mm, not 100: the rain at one point is recorded by the weather station.
  • Storm history — a chart over time: the environment’s instability, the updraught, the rotation, the rain. “CSV” exports it as a table.
  • The groups below: instability and shear indices (CAPE, CIN, SRH, SCP, STP…), a small thermodynamic diagram and the levels (LCL, LFC, EL, 0 °C), the hodograph and the shear (the circles at 0, 0.5, 1, 3, 6 and 10 km can be dragged with a mouse or a finger — the same wind as the sliders, and for a loaded sounding an adjustment of its wind between neighbouring circles; “Restore the wind” undoes it), “All the storm’s values” — with the chart “At what height the updraught rotates” and words: a mid-level mesocyclone, rotation below 3 km or a low-level mesocyclone; there also “Tornadogenesis signs” — a level (none / favourable environment / low-level mesocyclone / signs of tornadogenesis) and every condition with its value, and in “Thresholds” they can be changed.

6Radar

The “Radar” button (R) opens the Doppler radar screen; Esc or “Close” — back to 3D.

  • Products: Z — reflectivity (the echo’s strength), V — the velocity along the beam (green towards the radar, red away), SRV — the same with the storm’s motion taken out (a mesocyclone is a pair of green and red side by side), CR — the strongest echo over all tilts, ZDR, RHO (ρhv) and KDP — dual polarization (the shape and mix of particles: drops, hail, melting snow), and under the map — a vertical cross-section.
  • Tilt angle: a low one sees the precipitation near the ground, a high one the storm’s core and the anvil.
  • A click on the screen moves the radar there. Vertical cross-section — along any line A–B on the map of any product: the ends can be dragged, Shift + drag — a new line, Shift + click — from the radar through a point (like a real radar’s RHI); on a phone — “Draw a section” and two taps. The section shows the beams of the 14 tilts where they cross the line and the 0 and −20 °C isotherms; the section’s field — Z, V, SRV, ZDR, ρhv, KDP.
  • Scale and palette: a choice of ready palettes (colour-blind friendly ones too), “Edit” — your own colour editor, “Import .pal” and “Export .pal” — palette files of radar software.
  • Grid: data pixels (in radial cells as on a real radar — 0.5° or 1° beams — or smooth), radials, range rings and labels (degrees or compass points).
  • Mesocyclones (MDA): an automatic search for rotation in the velocity, as on NEXRAD radars, with the strength and a “hook” mark; next to it, for comparison, what the model itself computes.
  • Cursor: the azimuth, range, beam height and the value under the mouse.

7Skew-T, station, lightning, analysis

  • Skew-T (S) — a thermodynamic diagram: the temperature and dew point profile, the parcel’s path, shaded CAPE and CIN, the wind. A choice of parcel (surface-based, mixed layer, most unstable), its indices and levels. “Probe in the model” — a profile taken right from the model: in the selected cell’s inflow or in its core. The environment’s profile updates as the experiment runs: you see the sun eat the morning inversion.
  • Station (M) — virtual weather stations, up to four: press “Add with a click in 3D”, then click the ground — best in the storm’s path. Each station has its letter and colour, and a mast of that colour stands in the 3D view. The meteogram shows what an observer would see as the storm passes over: temperature, dew point, pressure, wind with gusts, precipitation. A letter chooses a station, “All” compares them on one chart: place a row along the storm’s path — the gust front reaches each in turn, and the time between the stations gives its speed. “CSV” — the records of the chosen station or of all. “From the ground” → “At the weather station” puts the observer at the chosen one.
  • Compare (C; the “Experiments” menu) — two runs side by side: let a storm develop, press “Remember this run as A”, change one setting — the new run becomes run B. The screen shows the strongest-echo maps of A and B at the same model minute, shared plots (A dashed, B solid), both runs’ highlights up to the same time and the settings that differ; A’s dashed line appears on the “Storm history” plots too. While A is remembered, restarts take its random seed — then the storms differ only by what was changed; with another seed and the same settings you see how much of a storm is chance. A is kept while the page is open.
  • Ensemble (E; the “Experiments” menu) — the current settings 5, 10 or 20 times for 30–120 minutes, each time with its own random seed and — by default — a small uncertainty of the starting data within the measurements’ error (the surface temperature, the humidity, the wind at its levels, the thermal’s place and strength), as in real ensembles; with the seed alone the members hardly part. The first member is the control: the data and seed of the run going on. Members run without the 3D view, one per core, several at once; the live run is paused meanwhile. The result: the share of members with a storm, a mesocyclone, hail of 20+ mm, surface wind of 20+ m/s, a downpour of 50+ mm/h, a total of 20+ mm — their probability in these conditions; the spread of the highlights (least · median · most); probability maps on the ground — rain, wind, the rotation track, hail; every member’s course and the median. CSV — every member’s highlights.
  • Regime map (K; the “Experiments” menu) — the run going on over a grid of conditions, like the Weisman and Klemp (1982) diagram: up, the humidity of the lowest 1.5 km (×0.8–1.1, and with it the CAPE); across, the 0–6 km wind shear (0–40 m/s; the hodograph is stretched about the ground wind, its shape kept). The seed and everything else are as in the run, so the cells differ only by the environment. A 3 × 3 or 4 × 5 grid, runs of 45–90 minutes — the same way as the ensemble’s. A cell is coloured by its regime: no storm, ordinary (it rose and decayed), multicell (the updraught still strong after the 40th minute — new cells rise), supercell (a mesocyclone held 10 min); inside, the updraught’s course. The ring is where the settings themselves stand. A click chooses a cell, “Take into the run” restarts the live run with its moisture and shear. CSV — every cell.
  • Lightning (L) — a lightning detector. The model’s lightning comes from physics: graupel and crystals charge in collisions, the charge builds up, and where the field exceeds the breakdown threshold a discharge goes; if the channel reaches the ground it is a ground stroke (CG), if not an in-cloud flash (IC). The first lightning usually comes 15–25 minutes into the storm. On the map: “−” and “+” are ground strokes (the usual and the positive kind), dots are in-cloud flashes, the colour is the age; “Density” — flashes per km² over 10, 30 or 60 minutes. The chart below — flashes in every minute; ▼ — a “lightning jump”, a sharp rise of the rate: the updraught is strengthening, often before hail and a squall. A click on a mark — the flash’s card. To turn lightning off (a faster run) — the “Lightning” box in the settings.
  • Analysis (A) — a surface analysis, like a forecaster’s map. The isobars are lines of equal pressure at the ground (every 0.5, 1 or 2 hPa depending on the range), every 5 hPa thicker; H and L are the centres of high and low pressure, the pressure under them. A blue line with triangles is a cold front, a red one with half-circles a warm front (the symbols point the way it moves). Green shading — rain at the ground. The stations (every 6 or 12 km): the temperature in red top left, the dew point in green bottom left, the pressure top right in tenths of hPa without the “10” or “9” (135 is 1013.5), the staff — where the wind blows from (a barb — 5 m/s, a half barb — 2.5, a pennant — 25), the circle filled by the cloud cover (in eighths), a triangle with a dot — a shower, “ϟ” — a thunderstorm within 10 km in 10 minutes. A click on a station — its numbers. The layers are switched with boxes. The pressure comes from the model itself (the weight of the air and the dynamic part). The domain is 50–150 km, so mesoscale detail shows: the mesohigh under a storm’s cold outflow (+1…+4 hPa), the pressure jump behind a cold front. A cyclone’s isobars do not fit here.

8Link, file, rewind

  • “Link to the run” copies an address with all the settings and the run’s random seed: anyone opening it gets the same run with the same numbers — handy for a class. A link works in both languages.
  • “Save” writes the whole run to a .meteolab file (~4 MB), “Open…” continues it from the same minute.
  • “Go back to minute…” — rewind: the state is kept every 5 model minutes; choose a minute and the run goes on from there again (you can change settings and see what changes).

9On a phone

  • 3D on the whole screen, at the top — the time, updraught, rain and rotation in large numbers and the storm mode.
  • The bottom bar: “Scenarios” (slides up a sheet with the scenarios and all the settings), “Pause”, “Restart”, “Speed” (×4 → ×8 → ×16 → ×30 → ×60), “Data” (a sheet with the numbers, charts and indices), “Instruments” (radar, Skew-T, zones, lightning, analysis, station, switching to the computer layout, this guide, the project’s channel, the language, the theme).
  • A sheet closes with the “Hide” button or a tap on the view behind it.
  • Gestures: a finger — rotate, a pinch — zoom, a double tap — a thermal. In the view from the ground a pinch zooms in and out.
  • The “Instruments” menu has “View from the ground”, “Full screen” and the picture quality.
  • The field panel (updraught, temperature and so on) above the view shows the name and the scale; the slices and volume are under “Settings ▾”.
  • In the instruments the screen is at the top, and the panel under it scrolls with a finger.

10Frequent questions

Nothing happens
wait 5–10 model minutes (T+ at the top) or raise the acceleration; in “Diurnal cycle” and “Loaded gun” the storm appears only after noon. If the “Starting thermal strength” slider is at zero and the “cap” is strong, convection may not start at all — that is a result too.
I move a slider and nothing changes
the “WK supercell” has its profile set by formulas, a loaded sounding by data: the temperature, humidity and wind sliders have no effect there (a sounding’s wind is adjusted on the hodograph). For the rest a change marked ↻ restarts the experiment — watch again from T+00:00.
Where to get a sounding
the easiest is the University of Wyoming site: choose a station and a time (00 or 12 UTC), “Text: List”, and copy the whole page into the “Real sounding…” window. SHARPpy files, input_sounding from CM1 or WRF and a CSV table with a header row work too. Samples, including the classic Weisman–Klemp profile, are in the soundings folder on GitHub. Data up to 10 km at least are needed.
It is slow
the run is computed on your computer. What helps: the standard domain instead of the large one, “Transport: fast”, “warm rain” microphysics, a smaller acceleration. The number of computing threads is shown at the top.
How to get a supercell
you need instability (CAPE from ~1.5 kJ/kg), strong 0–6 km wind shear (from ~20 m/s) and a wind veering with height near the ground (SRH 0–3 km from ~150–250; SRH 0–1 km matters already for tornadoes). Start with the “Classic supercell” and change one slider at a time.
Is this a weather forecast?
no — this is the experiment “a storm in a given environment”: the environment is the same over the whole domain, the ground is flat, the grid about a kilometre. What the model can and cannot do is in the article “About the model”.
Where to report a bug
