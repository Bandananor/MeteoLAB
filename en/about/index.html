About the model · StormLab

How a thunderstorm is computed

MeteoLab is a cloud-resolving model of deep convection computed right in the browser. You set profiles of temperature, humidity and wind by height and the surface type, and the model then solves the equations of motion, heat and water step by step in a three-dimensional domain the size of a large city. Everything on the screen — the cloud, the rain, the rotation, the gusts at the ground — follows from these equations; only detail smaller than ~1 km (billows, bands, fibres, rain streaks) is drawn in with noise tied to the model’s fields.

Important

The model is idealized: a horizontally uniform environment, flat ground, periodic edges, a grid spacing of about a kilometre. Some processes are simplified; everything known is listed in the “Limitations” section together with how it affects the numbers.

1How the model works

Domain and grid

ParameterValue
Domain48 × 36 × 18.9 km, periodic horizontally; the large one is 96 × 72 km at the same spacing, ~4 times slower
Grid40 × 32 × 50 nodes: Δx = 1.2 km, Δy = 1.125 km; vertically the spacing grows from 100 m at the ground to ~1 km at the top — 9 levels below 1 km
Time stepΔt = 3 s; the fall of precipitation is split into smaller steps if drops would fall more than a level in one step
Boundariesthe ground and the top are rigid walls; above max(tropopause + 2.5 km, 14 km) an absorbing layer damps the waves
Domain motionthe domain travels with the storm (the Bunkers right mover in strong shear, else the 0–6 km mean wind), so the storm does not leave over the edge; the ground, the precipitation, the radar and the station are tied to the ground

The background is a horizontally uniform environment from the sliders or a scenario: temperature by layers with an optional inversion, humidity by layers, wind at six heights. The pressure comes from hydrostatics. A profile from the sliders goes through a dry convective adjustment: wherever the air would overturn by itself (the virtual potential temperature decreasing with height), it is warmed slightly to neutral — otherwise instability would appear without any storm.

Equations

The core solves the Navier–Stokes equations in the form used in storm models (such as CM1 and WRF). The motion of the air:

∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = −∇(p′/ρ₀) + B·k − f·k×(u − u_g) + ∇·(K∇u)(1)
(u·∇)u
the transport of momentum by the wind itself
−∇(p′/ρ₀)
the pressure perturbation: the model finds it anew at every step from condition (2)
B·k
buoyancy, pointing up
f·k×(u − u_g)
the Coriolis force on the departure from the background wind (the “Latitude” slider)
∇·(K∇u)
turbulent mixing in place of molecular viscosity

The air is treated as anelastic: the mass flux is free of divergence, and the base-state density ρ₀(z) falls several times with height. There are no sound waves in such a solution, so the step can be seconds rather than fractions of a second.

∇·(ρ₀u) = 0(2)

The buoyancy accounts for the vapour, which is lighter than dry air, and the weight of all the water in the cloud — droplets, rain, ice, snow, graupel and hail:

B = g·[(θᵥ − θᵥ₀)/θᵥ₀ − q_c − q_r − q_i − q_s − q_g − q_h](3)

Here θᵥ = θ·(1 + 0.61·q_v) is the virtual potential temperature, θᵥ₀ its background value at the same height, q the mass fractions of cloud water, rain, cloud ice, snow, graupel and hail.

The potential temperature θ and the water fractions are carried by the same wind and mixed; θ changes with the latent heat of condensation, evaporation, freezing and melting. Turbulence below the grid scale follows Smagorinsky–Lilly: K = (C_s·Δ)²·√max(0, |S|² − N²/Pr), separately horizontally and vertically; in stable air it dies out. At the ground there is friction by the surface roughness.

The cold pool, the gust front and the microburst are not put in by formulas: they arise by themselves from the cooling by evaporating and melting precipitation, from the weight of the precipitation and from the pressure.

Numerical methods

  • Transport — in flux form, 5th-order WENO for water and a linear 5th-order scheme for the wind and θ, 3rd-order Runge–Kutta in time. The scheme conserves the water mass by itself; the fallback semi-Lagrangian transport is twice as fast but smears the updraughts.
  • Pressure — an exact projection: a two-dimensional fast Fourier transform horizontally and a tridiagonal solve vertically for each wave. After a step the divergence stays at the level of round-off.
  • The computation runs in a background thread of the browser and is shared between several processor cores through shared memory; a phone gets fewer threads.
import { AtmosphereModel, createGrid, DT } from './src/core'

const model = new AtmosphereModel(config, createGrid())  // 40 × 32 × 50, stretched in height
for (let s = 0; s < 3600 / DT; s++) { model.step(DT); model.time += DT }  // an hour of the model
console.log(model.rotation.uh, model.diagnostics().updraft)

2Clouds and precipitation

The microphysics is bulk: each kind of water is described by one quantity — its mass fraction. By default the model computes with ice.

  • Cloud. Vapour beyond saturation condenses at once, cloud in unsaturated air evaporates; below 0 °C saturation is taken over a mix of water and ice.
  • Rain after Kessler: cloud droplets merge into rain, rain collects cloud, falls at a speed depending on its amount and evaporates in dry air, cooling it.
  • Ice, snow and graupel after Lin, Farley and Orville (1983): crystals stick together into snow, snow gets rimed with supercooled droplets and becomes graupel, graupel melts into rain below the 0 °C level. Since 2.7.2 the ice comes from processes rather than a share by temperature: droplets stay liquid down to −38 °C and freeze at once there; crystals nucleate on ice nuclei (Cooper 1986) and grow at the droplets’ expense (the Bergeron process), and their number is a field of its own, fed by splinters from riming at −3…−8 °C (Hallett and Mossop 1974) and from freezing raindrops (Lawson et al. 2015). Crystals grow by habit (Koenig 1971: fastest at −15 °C, where dendrites grow), and the charging takes their number and size as plates.
  • Hail — separate dense ice: born from graupel when it collects more supercooled water than it can freeze, it grows and melts. At the ground the hail swath and the largest hailstones are computed — up to about 2 cm: a single-moment scheme gives no giant hail.

The “Warm rain” mode keeps only drops: cheaper, but no anvil of crystals and no latent heat of freezing.

3Surface and sun

The sun heats only the ground: the insolation depends on the sun’s height from the latitude, the day of the year and the local time. The surface energy balance divides the absorbed heat between heating the air, evaporation and storage in the soil or water; the share of evaporation depends on the surface type and the soil moisture. Grass, dry soil, water and city differ in roughness, albedo and moisture. Clouds do not block the sun, there is no night cooling: a start at dawn is set by the profile — a night inversion at the ground, which the sun then eats.

4How the model recognizes a storm

  • Environment indices — CAPE, CIN, LCL, LFC, EL for the surface-based, mixed-layer and most unstable parcels; DCAPE, SRH, SCP, STP. The parcel rises along the dry and the pseudo-moist adiabat and agrees with MetPy within ~1 % in CAPE.
  • Updraught rotation, UH — the integral of w·ζ over the 2–5 km layer; from 200 m²/s² the updraught is highlighted as rotating.
  • A mesocyclone is decided not by UH but by the rotation of the updraught core itself: the core’s mean vorticity at 2–5 km at least 3·10⁻³ s⁻¹ for 10 minutes. A pair of vortices of opposite sign on the flanks of an ordinary storm does not pass this criterion.
  • Cells — connected updraught cores where the updraught in cloud at 1.5–10 km is stronger than 4 m/s; each has a number, an origin, a motion and a stage: cumulus, mature, dissipating, supercell, occluding, left-moving supercell.
  • The radar computes reflectivity and Doppler velocity from the model’s fields at 14 beam tilts and finds mesocyclones in the manner of the MDA algorithm: the strength by the rotational velocity — weak from 12.5 m/s, moderate from 18, strong from 23.

5Tests

The computing core is checked by tests without a browser; long runs go in the GitHub Actions cloud at every change of the core.

  • Invariants: a background without a thermal stays the background for three hours; the water in a storm over an hour adds up with the rain that fell; no divergence remains after a step.
  • The parcel against MetPy 1.7.1: CAPE, CIN, LCL, LFC and EL on six profiles, from a summer day to a cap over a moist layer.
  • Benchmarks from the literature: the Straka cold bubble (1993) — the density current’s front, the coldest air and the wind; the Bryan and Fritsch warm bubble (2002) — dry and moist thermals.
  • The validation report builds itself: npm run validate runs every benchmark, the Weisman–Klemp experiment and the scenarios and writes a table of quantity — model — reference — bounds — source with the model’s version and the date; in the cloud, for every release. The latest — docs/validation.md. Where the model is off the reference (Straka’s front ~3 % behind), the report says so.
  • The Weisman–Klemp supercell: splits into a cyclonic right mover and an anticyclonic left mover; on the large domain the first 20 minutes are the same storm as on the standard one.
  • Scenarios: each with a starting thermal is run for 40 minutes; none hits the velocity limiter, a mesocyclone appears only where intended. The diurnal cycle — from 06:00 to the first storm; “Loaded gun” — from 09:00 to the cap breaking after 14:00.

7How MeteoLab is made

The project’s author is Daniil (Bandananor), who sets the tasks and goals, chooses the physics and its simplifications, checks the model against the literature and the tests, decides the releases and writes the paper. The code, the tests and the site’s texts are written mostly with an AI assistant (Claude by Anthropic, through Claude Code) on the author’s tasks. Every change of the physics comes with a measurement and a test, and the model is checked against benchmark cases from the literature (the validation report builds itself). The physics texts are also reviewed by an AI agent in the role of a meteorologist; that is no substitute for a review by a specialist.

The main points of versions 2.0–2.10.0. The full log with dates and measurements is in the README on GitHub (in Russian).

2.10.0.1 (22 Oct 2026): “Upper trough” recalibrated for the ice from processes: a colder trough (−4 K); without it the rain ends by 12:20, with it downpours go on almost to 13:00; all the synoptic situations are in the validation report.

2.10.0 (22 Oct 2026) “The laboratory”: the interface by task (menus, initial conditions, diagnostics as a table); comparing two runs, the ensemble, the CAPE × shear regime map, batch series from a file (in the browser and in Node); the fields as NetCDF; the version, seed and run link in every export, CITATION.cff; a validation report that builds itself (38 of 38 checks within bounds); vertical video with captions.

2.8.0 (19 Oct 2026) “How a storm works”: the outflow’s true cold (θv′); the supercell’s FFD and RFD downdraughts with their gust fronts and labels of the storm’s parts on the 3D view; the mesocyclone as a column; on the radar — TVS, VIL, echo tops, hail size (MESH) and velocity folding; a new “Satellite” instrument with visible and infrared channels and overshooting tops; “What to expect” — a forecast from the environment by the statistics of real storms beside what the model made; DCP and the effective inflow layer; the cloud genus of every cell.

2.7.2 (19 Oct 2026) “Ice and sprites”: ice comes from processes — droplets stay liquid down to −38 °C, crystals nucleate and grow by habit, splinters add to their number; supercells flash as in nature (~70 flashes a minute); sprites and halos above strong ground strokes at night and on the detector; SHIP; an honest tornado detector; the front on the analysis map by the edge of the cold air; WMO station symbols.

2.7.1 (19 Oct 2026) “English”: the English version of the site, the simulator and the guides (an RU / EN switch); with a front the station and the city stay on the ground; the captions “Rain in the core now” and “Total at the wettest point”; a camera in full screen; snapshots of the 3D view and the instruments; the cloud type and the rain and hail in words; a message instead of a black 3D view on phones; the snow under the anvil and the sun in a dense cloud made more plausible; texts from a meteorologist’s review.

2.7.0.1 (15 Oct 2026): fixes from testers’ feedback: rain falls in shafts from the cloud base, no shelf under the anvil, no coloured noise on phones and no “wallpaper” on the anvil from above, the “Instruments” menu on a phone scrolls.

2.7.0 (15 Oct 2026) “True to life”: the picture reworked — the sky and the time of day from atmospheric physics (sunset, twilight, a night with stars), lightning with branching and subsequent strokes, the shape of storms (billows, the anvil, banded flanks, mammatus), rain shafts and a rainbow, wet ground and hail on the ground, a view from the ground with a choice of place, full screen, quality levels, a radar cross-section through two points. Detail smaller than 1 km, the shadows and the night are only a picture and do not affect the model.

2.6.1 (13 Oct 2026): fixes from a realism review: the pressure on the surface analysis comes from the model itself (it used to be 2–4 times too high under a storm), the map’s symbols by the WMO, honest texts on the model’s limits in the situations.

2.6 (13 Oct 2026): advection — the environment changes by itself: over hours the wind brings warm, cold, moist or dry air into a chosen layer, with a chart of CAPE and CIN by the hour. Synoptic situations — 2–3 hour stories (“Warm sector and cold front”, “Upper trough”, “Warm air above the layer: no storms”) and a builder for your own situation. The “Analysis” instrument — a forecaster’s surface map: isobars, H and L, the front, stations.

2.5 (13 Oct 2026): fronts — cold or warm, with the air masses’ contrast, the depth and slope of the frontal surface, the speed and heading: a cold wedge lifts the warm air and breaks the cap, a new “Cold front” scenario. 2.4 (13 Oct 2026): environment zones on the map — patches of ground where the air is warmer or colder, moister or drier: a storm passing through them strengthens or weakens by itself, a breeze is born at the edges of warm zones; the storms’ paths on the map, a zone’s CAPE and CIN before the run.
